How to Calculate Steel Weight

Steel weight is determined by multiplying the volume of the section by the density of the material. For mild steel, the internationally accepted density is 7850 kg/m³ (7.85 g/cm³). Every calculator on this page uses the following core relationship:

Weight (kg) = Volume (mm³) × Density (kg/m³) ÷ 1,000,000,000

Volume is obtained by multiplying the cross-sectional area of the profile by its length. The only part that changes between a plate, an angle, a pipe or a beam is how that cross-sectional area is derived — which is exactly what this tool automates for you.

MS Sheet and Plate Weight

A sheet or plate is a simple rectangular solid, so the volume is length × width × thickness. For example, a standard 2440 × 1220 × 6 mm MS plate works out to roughly 140 kg. In Indian trade practice, material under 5 mm thickness is normally called a sheet, while 5 mm and above is called a plate. Common commercial sheet sizes are 8′×4′ (2440 × 1220 mm) and 6′×3′ (1830 × 915 mm).

MS Angle Weight

An angle's cross-sectional area is calculated as (A + B − T) × T, where A and B are the two leg lengths and T is the thickness. The thickness is subtracted once because the corner region would otherwise be counted twice — this is the single most common mistake people make when calculating angle weight by hand. A 50 × 50 × 6 mm angle in a standard 6-metre length weighs approximately 26.6 kg.

Pipe and Hollow Section Weight

For any hollow section, subtract the inner area from the outer area before multiplying by length. For round pipe the formula is π × (R² − r²), where R is the outer radius and r is the inner radius. A 60 mm OD pipe with 3 mm wall thickness weighs about 4.22 kg per running metre. For square and rectangular tube, the equivalent is (W × H) − ((W − 2T) × (H − 2T)).

Channel and Beam Weight

ISMC channels and ISMB beams are treated as a web plus two flanges: (H − 2Tf) × Tw + 2 × W × Tf. This gives a close approximation, typically within 2–4% of the value published in IS 808, because it ignores the root radius and the flange taper. For exact structural calculations, always refer to the IS 808 section tables or the mill test certificate.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does the actual delivered weight differ from the calculated weight?
Rolling mills work within tolerance limits defined by standards such as IS 1852 and IS 1730. Real sections vary by roughly ±2–3% because of thickness variation across the width, mill scale, rounded corners and flange taper. Always treat calculator output as an estimate for costing and load planning, not as a certified figure.

What is the density of mild steel?
7850 kg/m³. Stainless steel 304 is slightly denser at about 8000 kg/m³, aluminium is far lighter at 2700 kg/m³, and cast iron sits around 7200 kg/m³. You can switch between all of these using the Material dropdown at the top of this page.

Can I use this calculator for stainless steel or aluminium?
Yes. Select the relevant material from the dropdown and the density is applied automatically to every shape.

Does it work in inches and pounds?
Yes. Switch Units to Imperial and enter all dimensions in inches — the results are then shown in pounds.

Is my data sent anywhere?
No. All calculations run entirely inside your browser. Saved calculations are stored only in your own device's local storage and can be deleted at any time with the Clear All button.
